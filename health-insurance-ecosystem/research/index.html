Health Insurance Ecosystem — Research Hub

Three 10-tab pages from one corpus and one voice router, first researched 2026-09-25, corpus doubled and every tab re-measured 2026-10-07, ahead of Medicare AEP (Oct 15–Dec 7) and ACA open enrollment (Nov 1–Jan 15). The agent page is the business buyer; the Medicare and ACA pages are the consumers agents sell to. Every document belongs to exactly one page.

Independent Medicare & ACA agents

The business buyer: the agent or broker who buys leads, transfers and appointments.

4,220 buyer documents

  • Who: 4,220 first-person agent documents (4,092 health / general agents, 76 life or final-expense agents talking about buying leads, 52 new or aspiring agents) from r/InsuranceAgent, agent Facebook groups, Insurance-Forums.com and agent YouTube. Property & casualty agents, call-center staff, vendors and recruiters are excluded.
  • The lead is the whole job: Previous Solutions is the largest tab (440 documents bought leads from vendors, 141 aged leads, 164 transfers and inbound calls, 124 self-generated), and the top frustration after MLM recruiting is leads that are old, fake, shared or never answer (124 documents).
  • What they pay (published prices, median of the vendors' ranges, Medicare Advantage): aged records $1.00–$6.50, shared $15–$31, exclusive $35–$75, T65 $45–$90, live transfers / inbound calls $58–$109 per call. The Lead Sources tab lists every price agents wrote themselves, with its link.
  • What an enrollment is worth, and what each lead type costs to get one: $725 for a new Medicare Advantage enrollment in 2027 (CMS maximum), $363 a year after; Medicare Supplement about $502 in year one; ACA $20–$30 per member per month. At published rates one MA enrollment costs the agent $48–$312 from aged data, $233–$437 from transfers, $516–$1,106 from exclusive leads and $1,958–$3,982 from shared leads. Self-generated Meta leads (the agent's own campaign, exclusive): $764–$2,484 at the published stage benchmarks, $165 in ads at one agent's measured T65 campaign (352 leads, 12 enrollments and rising) (Metrics tab).

Medicare shoppers (AEP)

The person turning 65 or already on Medicare, and the family helping. Buyer and user are the same person.

10,534 buyer documents

  • Who: 10,534 first-person Medicare shoppers (10,327 turning 65 or on Medicare, 207 family members helping), mostly comment threads under 90 Medicare explainer videos plus r/medicare. Agents, SHIP counselors, former brokers and commenters posting links are excluded.
  • The decision is Original Medicare + Medigap versus Medicare Advantage, and the fear is on the Advantage side: networks, prior authorization and denials (65 fear documents; 481 complaint documents), a big illness and its bills (65), and being stuck after failing underwriting (35; 213 in the complaints ledger).
  • The frustration is the system itself: too confusing to compare (456), premiums that keep going up (162), Part D changes (70) and doctors or hospitals dropping Advantage plans (45).
  • What they did: 1,180 documents hold or held an Advantage plan and 704 a Plan G or N; 166 went through an agent or broker; 57 got out of Advantage onto a supplement. Both sides have satisfied voices: 319 say Advantage works for them, 190 describe peace of mind with a supplement.

ACA marketplace shoppers (OEP)

The individual or family buying Obamacare coverage. Buyer and user are the same person.

1,793 buyer documents

  • Who: 1,793 first-person marketplace shoppers from r/Obamacare, r/HealthInsurance and the comment threads under early-retirement, self-employed and 2026-premium videos. Employer-plan stories, agents, vendors and political comments with no personal plan detail are excluded, so the voice is thinner than the Medicare page.
  • What they fear: the premium at renewal (36 documents), getting sick or hurt and not being able to pay (24), and the income math: the cliff and paying credits back at tax time (19).
  • What changed: the enhanced premium tax credits expired for 2026; CMS reports 23.1M plan selections (down 4.9%) and about 1.5M improper enrollments removed. The complaint ledger is led by premiums that doubled or tripled (105 documents), the cliff and repayment (91) and deductibles too high to use the plan (15).
  • What they do about it: pick a tier (109 documents name bronze, silver or gold), go to HealthCare.gov or the state exchange themselves (104), weigh COBRA or a spouse's plan (105), move to Medicaid (101), or go without (40). Only 24 name a broker or agent.
